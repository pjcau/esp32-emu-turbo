# Arcade (MAME)

Full MAME is far too large for a microcontroller, but the classic 8-bit
arcade boards are within reach, the same class of work as the home
consoles in [More Systems](/docs/next-steps/more-systems).

| Tier | Boards | State |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 🟢 **Runs at full speed** | Pac-Man, 1942, 1943, Robby Roto, the Exidy boards and similar Z80 / 6502 / 8080 boards of 1978–85 | 57–60 fps |
| 🟡 **Runs near full speed, a third of the frames drawn** | 68000 boards: Seibu (Blood Bros.), Video System (Aero Fighters), Capcom CPS1, Sega System 16, Neo Geo MVS (unencrypted sets up to ~50 MB) | 45–60 game frames a second, 15–21 drawn |
| 🔴 **No** | CPS2, encrypted Neo Geo sets (KOF '99 and later), boards with two 68000 (OutRun: played through [Cannonball](../software/overview.md) instead), 3D boards | CPU load, encryption this MAME version lacks, 3D hardware |

## Where we are (2026-10-04)

The arcade emulator is **`mame-go`**: MAME 0.37b5 (the mame2000 code base)
compiled with only the chosen drivers, a retro-go app on its own 2 MB
partition. Launcher tabs **Arcade (MAME)** (`/sd/roms/arcade/`), **Neo Geo**
(`/sd/roms/neogeo/`, with `neogeo.zip`) and **Capcom CPS-1** (`/sd/roms/cps1/`).

| Board family | Games checked on the board | In play |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Namco / Capcom / Exidy / Bally 8-bit | Pac-Man, 1942, 1943, Robby Roto, Targ, Spectar, Side Trak, Hard Hat, Circus, Crash, Rip Cord, Robot Bowl, Starfire, Fire One | 57–60 fps (1943: about 25 drawn) |
| Galaga, Donkey Kong (Jr, 3), Galaxian, Moon Cresta, Frogger, Scramble | compiled, never tested: no ROMs at hand | |
| Seibu, Video System (68000) | Blood Bros. 60 fps; Aero Fighters 47–57 | about 20 drawn |
| Neo Geo MVS | 16 sets load; Metal Slug 58 fps / 20 drawn; Metal Slug 2 46 / 16 | sprites and large samples paged from the card |
| Capcom CPS1 | Final Fight 55 / 18; Street Fighter II CE 46 / 15; Carrier Air Wing, Ghouls'n Ghosts, Knights of the Round start and save | |
| Sega System 16 | Golden Axe 50 / 17; Alien Syndrome 50 / 17; Wonder Boy III 49 / 16 | first runs on 2026-10-04; Shinobi, Altered Beast not tried |

What stops the 68000 boards short of 60 drawn frames, and what was tried, is
in the [Arcade 60 fps plan](arcade-60fps-plan.md).

What the app does besides running the games:

- **ROM sets** are matched to a driver by file names and CRCs, so modern sets
  with renamed files load, whatever the zip is called. Sets MAME 0.37b5 does
  not know do not (Fantasy Zone II `fantzn2x`, encrypted Neo Geo sets).
- **Save states** from the retro-go menu and *Resume* from the launcher. A
  state loads only in the firmware that wrote it. On the Neo Geo a save has
  no preview picture (no screenshot in its band mode).
- **Hiscores** on the card (`hiscore.dat` in `/sd/retro-go/mame/mame2000/`).
- **Loading percentage** under the hourglass; after another arcade game was
  played, "game cache NN%" while the flash cache is rewritten.
- **Launch times**: 9 to 13 s for the same game again, 20 to 28 s after a
  different arcade game (the 4 MB flash cache holds one game). The first
  launch of a Neo Geo game converts its sprites to the card (minutes).
- **Audio at 32000 Hz** like every app; SELECT is the coin, START is start.

## What was done, in short

1. **The app** (2026-09-26): MAME 0.37b5 with selected drivers; driver tables
   `const` in flash so they cost no internal RAM; sets matched by CRC.
2. **16-bit boards**: graphics larger than 256 KB decoded on demand into a
   tile cache instead of expanded in memory; ROM regions of 256 KB and more
   served memory-mapped from a 4 MB flash partition; the 68000's vblank wait
   loops skipped.
3. **Neo Geo** (2026-09-27): sprites converted once to a file on the card and
   read through a page cache, samples in flash or paged from the card, so
   games of 30 to 50 MB fit in 8 MB of PSRAM; exact save states.
4. **CPS1** (2026-09-27/29): graphics streamed from the zip into the flash
   cache; sound board on the second core.
5. **Speed** (2026-10-02/04): the [Arcade 60 fps plan](arcade-60fps-plan.md):
   band rendering, branch-free display scaler, exact idle skips, 16 kHz sound
   mix, the CPS1's frame handed to the display task.
6. **Sega System 16** (2026-10-04): a subset of the 0.37b5 driver (one 68000,
   Z80, YM2151), generated by `mame-go/tools/system16_subset.py`.

The dated record of all of this, with every measurement, is in the
repository: `docs/archived/arcade-mame-log.md`.

## How it fits the console

- **Flash:** the app has a 2 MB partition (1.93 MB used) and a 4 MB data
  partition, `mamerom`, for the large read-only regions of the game being
  played. The 16 MB are full: 64 KB left.
- **PSRAM (8 MB):** program, work memory and caches; graphics and samples
  too large for it are paged from the card (Neo Geo) or kept in the flash
  partition.
- **Internal RAM** is the scarce resource: 0 to 5 KB free while Metal Slug
  runs, which is why driver tables are `const` and the whole component's
  `.bss` is in PSRAM.
- **Controls:** the 12 buttons cover joystick, 1–3 fire buttons, coin
  (SELECT) and START.
- **Proof of a change:** pictures and sound compared frame by frame on a PC
  harness (`scripts/neogeo_frames.py`), then measured on the board.

## Board families, not single games

Every arcade game runs on a specific board, and many games share the same
one: the Capcom CPS1 board, for example, runs Street Fighter II, Final
Fight, Ghouls'n Ghosts and dozens more. **Feasibility is decided per board
family**; if v3 runs the board, it runs nearly all of its games. The few
exceptions inside a family are ROM sets too large for the PSRAM or a
special chip on one title.

**How to tell which board and CPU a game uses:** look the game up on the
Arcade Database (adb.arcadeitalia.net) or the Killer List of Videogames
(arcade-museum.com), or run `mame -listxml <game>`. Rule of thumb: a Z80,
6502, 8080 or 6809 at up to ~4 MHz, 1978–85, is classic and fits v3; a
68000 (CPS1, Sega System 16, Neo Geo) is the 16-bit tier, at the limit or
beyond.
